## Narrative Arc

The defense should tell a **single coherent story** with a clear villain, hero, and resolution:

- **Villain:** Real-world heterogeneous multi-agent systems (drone swarms, mixed fleets) are computationally expensive to train, brittle across team compositions, and architecturally rigid.
- **Hero:** Careful *representational design* eliminates most of these barriers without adding algorithmic complexity.
- **Resolution:** The DIE-HARL framework — Durable (C3), Implicit (C2), Extensible (C1) — gives a principled, practical path forward.

Each contribution should feel like it naturally raises the question answered by the next.

### SECTION 3 — C1: Policy Upsampling (Curriculum-Based Team Scaling)
**Slides: 7 | Time: ~12 min**
*Corresponds to dissertation Ch. 3 (Contribution 1)*

#### Slide 3.1 — C1 Motivation: The Smit et al. (2023) Inspiration
- Prior work scaled football teams 2v2 → 11v11 via curriculum.
- Key finding: full-team training produced better role differentiation; but cost and generalization were challenges.
- C1 hypothesis: does smaller-team pretraining + policy duplication reduce training cost without sacrificing performance?
- Figure: reference to Smit's football environment (if image available); otherwise descriptive text.

#### Slide 3.2 — C1 Research Questions & Method
- RQ1 and RQ2 (listed concisely).
- Training pipeline: Phase 1 (pretrain $k$ agents) → Phase 2 (upsample to $N$ agents, continue training).
- Observation schema design: **truncated** and **ally-ignorant** — how they allow a fixed network size across team sizes.
- Figure: training phase diagram (from `training_1.png` / `training_2.png` if available in figures dir)

#### Slide 3.3 — The Agent-Steps Metric
- Why standard metrics fail (wall-clock varies; training steps obscure agent-count cost).
- Definition: agent-steps = agent count × training steps.
- Validation: Pearson ρ > 0.999.
- Figure: `iter_cost.png` — show the linear scaling plot.
- **Speaker note:** This metric is a contribution in its own right; it enables every cross-configuration comparison in C1.

#### Slide 3.4 — Waterworld Results
- Environment: continuous control, homogeneous structure, low coordination, behavioral heterogeneity.
- Finding: pretraining accelerates convergence; benefit scales with size ratio; diminishing returns after ~60 steps.
- Figure: `Waterworld-AUCs.png`
- Take-away box: *"Clear curriculum win — low coordination means small-team policy transfers cleanly."*

#### Slide 3.5 — Multiwalker Results
- Environment: tight temporal coordination, sparse rewards, fixed spatial roles (static intrinsic heterogeneity).
- Finding: curriculum as *stabilization*, not just speed — prevents catastrophic early failure in large teams.
- Figure: `Multiwalker-AUCs.png`
- Take-away box: *"Task structure determines the nature of the benefit."*

#### Slide 3.6 — Level-Based Foraging (LBF) Results
- Environment: discrete gridworld, dynamic intrinsic heterogeneity (skill distribution shifts with team size).
- Finding: limited and inconsistent benefit; dynamic heterogeneity creates coordination challenges that don't transfer.
- Schema comparison: Full ≫ ally-ignorant (p < 0.001); Full ≈ truncated.
- Figure: `LBF-AUCs.png`
- Take-away box: *"When heterogeneity itself changes with team size, policy duplication cannot bridge the gap."*

#### Slide 3.7 — C1 Discussion & Bridge to C2
- Summary table: Environment / Outcome / Driver (3 rows).
- Cross-cutting insight: in every case, the bottleneck is *representational* — either the schema prevents transfer or the heterogeneity structure changes with team size.
- Bridge line: *"If we design observation spaces to naturally support structural heterogeneity, we eliminate these barriers at the source. That is C2."*

### SECTION 4 — C2: Implicit Indication via Homogenization
**Slides: 8 | Time: ~13 min**
*Corresponds to dissertation Ch. 4 (Contribution 2)*

#### Slide 4.1 — C2 Motivation: From C1 to C2
- C1 lesson: observation-space coupling is the primary obstacle to policy reuse.
- Existing HARL solutions: one-hot IDs (brittle) or per-type policies (|I|× storage, no cross-type generalization).
- C2 proposal in one sentence: construct a *homogenized* observation space; let observation *pattern* indicate agent type implicitly.

#### Slide 4.2 — C2 Research Questions
- RQ1: Learning efficiency with partially overlapping observation spaces?
- RQ2: Stability under team-size changes and partial observation loss?
- RQ3: Computational/implementation cost vs. benefit?

#### Slide 4.3 — HyperGrid: Purpose-Built Evaluation Environment
- Why a new environment: existing benchmarks lack configurable sensor heterogeneity.
- Design: n-dimensional MiniGrid; agent type = sensor channel configuration.
- Four training configurations: Complete, Intersecting-span, Disjoint-span, Incomplete.
- Figure: `marl-minigrid.png`
- **Speaker note:** The environment isolates observation structure as the independent variable — critical for controlled comparison in C3.

#### Slide 4.4 — The Implicit Indication Framework
- Homogenized space: $\tilde{\mathcal{O}} = \bigcup_{i \in I} \mathcal{O}_i$; unused elements masked to zero.
- Single shared policy $\pi_\theta(\cdot \mid \tilde{o})$ — identity inferred from populated/masked pattern.
- Required condition: **semantic decomposability** — observation elements must have type-consistent meanings.
- Small channel-assignment table (Agent types A/B/C × Channels R/G/B).
- Theoretical grounding: Deep Sets (Zaheer 2017) — the single-policy is at least as expressive as the per-type collection.

#### Slide 4.5 — Training Results: Matching HAPPO at 1/|I| Cost
- Comparison: Implicit Indication vs. HAPPO (separate actor + critic per agent type).
- Finding: matched performance across all four sensor configurations with 1/|I| storage.
- Disjoint-span shows strongest relative performance (reduced gradient interference).
- Figure: training curves plot (from `training_curves.png` if available)
- Take-away: *"Same reward, a fraction of the storage."*

#### Slide 4.6 — Robustness Evaluation: Eight Perturbation Conditions
- Agents evaluated on configurations they were NOT trained for.
- List the 8 conditions (sensor loss, degradation, improvement, coverage reduction/expansion, shuffled assignments, incomplete generalization, zero-shot novel type).
- Figure: `performance_delta.png`
- Key result: Implicit Indication handles novel configurations without explicit robustness training.

#### Slide 4.7 — C2 Summary Table
- Properties vs. Results table: Performance / Storage / Zero-shot / Sensor tolerance / Team-size flexibility / Requirement.
- This slide should feel like a "proof of concept complete" moment.

#### Slide 4.8 — Bridge to C3
- Natural question the committee will have: *"This representational approach looks good — but what about purpose-built architectural solutions like GNNs? Is simpler actually better?"*
- Bridge line: *"C3 answers that directly with a controlled head-to-head comparison."*

### SECTION 5 — C3: Architectural vs. Representational Approaches
**Slides: 6 | Time: ~10 min**
*Corresponds to dissertation Ch. 5 (Contribution 3)*

#### Slide 5.1 — Two Paths to Invariance
- Side-by-side: Representational (C2 approach) vs. Architectural (PIC — Permutation Invariant Critic, Liu 2020).
- Comparison table: Mechanism / Agent info / Network type / Complexity / Training algorithm.
- Prior expectation callout: *"The more sophisticated architectural approach should win."*
- Matched network capacities and identical hyperparameter sweeps — only the invariance mechanism differs.

#### Slide 5.2 — C3 Research Questions
- RQ1: Learning efficiency: GNN vs. representational?
- RQ2: Robustness under partial observability and team composition changes?
- RQ3: Computational/implementation cost vs. benefit?

#### Slide 5.3 — Performance Results: The Surprise
- Headline: **The simpler approach wins — consistently and substantially.**
- Results table (4 configurations × 2 methods × mean return):
  - Complete: 9.10 vs. 3.89 (2.34×)
  - Intersecting: 5.04 vs. 1.61 (3.13×)
  - Disjoint: 5.80 vs. 1.84 (3.15×)
  - Incomplete: 4.63 vs. 1.05 (4.41×)
- Key observation: the advantage *grows* as observability is constrained.
- Figure: `eval_scale.png`

#### Slide 5.4 — Robustness: Implicit Indication Dominates
- Implicit Indication outperforms PIC across all 8 robustness conditions.
- Also outperforms HAPPO under perturbation — a single shared policy beats both alternatives.
- Figures: `eval_heatmaps.png` + `eval_boxes.png` (side-by-side or sequential)

#### Slide 5.5 — Why Representation Wins: Mechanistic Explanation
- PIC aggregates via symmetric graph pooling — appropriate when *relational structure between agents is rich*.
- In HyperGrid, coordination is mediated by the task environment, not direct agent-to-agent relationships.
- Explicit masking gives a **cleaner gradient signal** — the MLP exploits the populated/masked structure directly.
- GNN aggregation may *smooth away* exactly the structure that encodes identity.
- Key principle box: *"Match the invariance mechanism to the structure of the problem, not to the expressive power of the architecture."*

#### Slide 5.6 — C3 Summary
- One-sentence version of each finding (training, evaluation, robustness).
- Transition to synthesis: *"Three contributions, three results — all pointing the same direction."*

### SECTION 6 — Synthesis & Cross-Cutting Insights
**Slides: 3 | Time: ~5 min**
*Corresponds to dissertation Ch. 6 §3*

#### Slide 6.1 — What's Unique? *(Advisor-requested)*
- Bulleted list of the novel contributions of the dissertation as a whole — the "so what" for each contribution and for the work collectively.
- Suggested content (to be refined against the actual novelty statements in each contribution chapter):
  - **C1:** First systematic evaluation of curriculum-based team scaling across *heterogeneous* MARL environments; introduction of agent-steps as a normalized training cost metric; identification of observation schema design as the gating factor for policy transfer.
  - **C2:** First formalization of *implicit indication* — agent identity conveyed through homogenized observation structure rather than explicit labels; theoretical guarantee (via Deep Sets) that the single shared policy is at least as expressive as the per-type collection; demonstrated robustness to sensor dropout, team-size change, and zero-shot novel compositions without retraining.
  - **C3:** First controlled empirical comparison of architectural (GNN/PIC) vs. representational (implicit indication) invariance paradigms under matched conditions in HARL; finding that representational approaches outperform architectural ones by 2.34–4.41× across all configurations.
  - **Collectively:** A unified representational framework (DIE-HARL) providing practical design principles for efficient, flexible, and robust heterogeneous multi-agent systems.
- ⚠️ **Note for drafting:** Verify these novelty claims against the "Contribution" or "Summary" sections of each chapter `.tex` file before finalizing. Advisors often have specific language they want used here.
- **Speaker note:** This slide answers "why does this matter and what's new?" Keep energy high — this is a strong moment.

#### Slide 6.2 — Four Cross-Cutting Insights
- Numbered list (keep to 4, not 5 — tight):
  1. **Representation Over Architecture** — simpler representational solutions outperform complex architectures (C2, C3).
  2. **Task Structure as Critical Moderator** — no universal method; characteristics determine what works (C1).
  3. **Unified Sharing Without Interchangeability** — semantic decomposability unlocks parameter sharing across structural heterogeneity (C2, C3).
  4. **Emergent Robustness** — deployment flexibility comes for free from good representational design (C2, C3).
- **Speaker note:** These four insights are the take-home value for anyone building real heterogeneous systems.

#### Slide 6.3 — Practitioner Decision Framework *(optional; include if time permits)*
- Simple decision tree or 2×2 matrix:
  - Behavioral heterogeneity → Curriculum/pretraining (C1)
  - Structural/observation heterogeneity → Homogenization (C2, C3)
  - Rich relational agent interactions → Consider architectural (GNN) augmentation
  - Semantically decomposable observations → Implicit Indication

### Speaker notes
- Use `\note{...}` at the end of each frame for full-paragraph notes.
- Use `\note[item]{...}` to add individual bullet-point notes.
- Enable two-screen layout during practice with `\setbeameroption{show notes on second screen=right}`.
- Every drafted frame should include a `\note{}` — even a brief one.
